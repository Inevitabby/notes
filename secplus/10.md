---
title: "Third-Party Vendor Risks"
---

Potential security and operational challenged introduced by external entities (vendors, suppliers, or service suppliers).

Types:
* Hardware (Conduct rigorous supply chain assessments).
* Software (Easier to review if open-source).
* Managed Service Providers (MSPs); things to consider:
	* Evaluate data security measures
	* Ensure confidentiality and integrity
	* Assess cybersecurity protocols
	* Response to security breach

# Supply Chain Attacks

Attack that involves targeting a weaker link in the supply chain to gain access to a primary target.
* You are only as strong as the weakest link in the supply chain (e.g., preinstalled malware).

CHIPS Act: US federal statue that provides roughly 280 billion to boost research and manufacturing of semiconductors in the US. Goal is to reduce supply chain risk in semiconductors.

Defense:
1. Vendor Due Diligence
2. Regular Monitoring and Audits of Supply Chain
3. Education and Collaboration
4. Incorporating Contractual Safeguards

# Vendor Assessment

Process that organizations implement to evaluate security, reliability, and performance of external entities.

* **Vendors**: Businesses or individuals that provide goods or services to an organization.
* **Suppliers**: Individuals involved in the production and delivery of products or parts of products.
* **Managed Service Providers**: Individuals hired by companies to manage IT services on behalf of an organization.

Penetration Testing: Simulated cyberattack against suppliers system to check for exploitable vulnerabilities.
* Right to audit clause grants organizations right to evaluate vendors.

Internal Audit: Vendor's self assessment where they evaluate their own practices against industry standards or organizational requirements.

Independent Assessment: Evaluation conducted by third-party entities that have no stake in the organization's or vendor's operations.

# Vendor Selection and Monitoring

Due Diligence: Rigorous evaluation that goes beyond surface-level credentials; checks:
* Financial stability
* Operational history
* Client testimonials
* On the ground practices

Conflict of Interest: When personal or financial relationships could potentially cloud the judgement of individuals involved in vendor selection. Organizations should always strive to achieve transparency.

Vendor Questionnaires: Comprehensive documents that potential vendors fill out to offer insights into the operations, capabilities, and compliance. (e.g., asking data redundancy, security protocols, uptime guarantees, disasters recovery plans).

Rules of Engagement: Guidelines that dictate terms of interaction between an organization and its potential vendors. (e.g., communication protocols, data sharing policies, negotiation boundaries).

Monitoring: Mechanism to ensure that chosen vendor still aligns with organizational needs and standards.

Feedback Loops: Two-way communication channel where both organization and vendor share feedback.

# Contracts and Agreements

* **Basic Contract**: Versatile tool that formally establishes a relationship between two parties.
* **Service-Level Agreement (SLA)**: Standard of service a client can expect from a provider. (e.g., no more than 2 hours of downtime per-month; deviation results in penalties).
* **Memorandum of Agreement (MOA)**: Formal and outlines specific responsibilities and roles of the involved parties. (e.g., who does content creation, who does distribution, who funds the campaign).
* **Memorandum of Understanding (MOU)**: Less binding and more declaration of mutual intent. Usually to signal intent to work together in the future without laying down specific details.
* **Master Service Agreement (MSA)**: Blanket agreement that covers general terms of engagement between parties across multiple transactions. Useful for a recurring client relationship when you do not want to draft a new contract for every project.
* **Statement of Work (SOW)**: Used to specify details for a particular project. Contains deliverable, timelines, and milestones.
* **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)**: Commitment to privacy that ensures that any sensitive information shared during negotiations remains confidential between both parties.
* **Business Partnership Agreement (BPA)**: Document that goes a step beyond the basic contract when two entities decide to pool their resources for mutual benefit.
