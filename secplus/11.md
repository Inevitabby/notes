GRC Triad: Governance, Risk, and Compliance.

# Governance

Governance: Strategic leadership, structures, and processes that ensure an organizations IT infrastructure aligns with its business objectives.
* Includes the rules, responsibilities, and practices that guide an organization in achieving its goals and managing IT resources.
* Crucial for:
	1. **Risk Management**: Helps in identifying, assessing, and managing potential risks that could impact organizational security.
	2. **Strategic Alignment**: Ensures organizations IT strategy aligns with its business objectives.
	3. **Resource Management**: Helps in efficient and effective use of IT resources.
	4. **Performance Measurement**: Provides mechanisms for measuring and monitoring IT performance processes.

Monitoring: Regularly reviewing and assessing the effectiveness of the governance framework.

Revision: Updating the governance framework to address these gaps or weaknesses.

> You must use monitoring and governance to stay up-to-date with regulatory, cultural, and technological changes.

# Governance Structures

Board of Directories: Group of individuals elected by shareholders to oversee the management of an organization.

Committee: Subgroups of a board of directories each with a specific focus (e.g., cybersecurity committee).

Government Entities: Establish laws and regulations that organizations must comply with (e.g., FTC).

Centralized Structures: Decision-making authority concentrated at the top levels of management.

Decentralized Structures: Distributes decision-making authority throughout the organization.

> Whether an organization is centralized or decentralized depends largely on the organization, goals, and size.

# Policies

## Acceptable Use Policy (AUP)
Outlines dos and don'ts for users interacting with an organizations IT systems and resources.

## Information Security Policies
Outline how an organization protects its information assets from internal and external threats.
* Covers data classification, access control, encryption, and physical security.

## Business Continuity
Focuses on how an organization continues critical operations during and after a disruption.

## Disaster Recovery
Focused specifically on how an organization will recovers its IT systems and data after a disaster.

## Incident Response
Plan for handling security incidents.
* Helps organizations respond quickly and effectively, minimizing damage and downtime.

## Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC)
Guides how software is developed within an organization.

## Change Management
Aims to ensure that changes are implemented in a controlled and coordinated manner, minimizing risk of disruptions.

# Standards

Provide a framework for implementing security measures, ensuring all aspects of an organizations security posture are addressed.

Password Standards: Dictate complexity and management of passwords.
* e.g., minimum length, mix of characters, rotation, password hashing and salting.

Access Control Standards: Determine who has access to what in an organization.
* Discretionary Access Control (DAC): Allows owner to decide who can access it.
* Mandatory Access Control (MAC): Uses labels to determine access (usually used in government and military).
* Role-Biased Access Control (RBAC): Access based on roles in an organization.

> These standards may also cover principles of least privilege and separation of duties.

Physical Security Standards: Physical measures taken to protect and organizations assets and information.
* Also covers environmental controls like fire suppression, HVAC, and power redundancy systems.

Encryption Standards: Ensure that intercepted data remains unreadable and secure.
* Choice of standard depends on specific use case and balance between security and performance.

# Procedures

Systematic sequences of actions or steps taken to achieve a specific outcome.

Emergency Evacuation Procedure: Outlines steps employees should take in case of an emergency, such as fire or natural disaster.

Data Backup Procedure: Details how and when data should be backed up to prevent data loss.

Change Management: Systematic approach to dealing with changes within an organization.
1. Need for change is identified, potential impacts assessed.
2. Plan is developed.
3. Change is implemented in stages.
4. A review is conducted.

Onboarding: Process of integrating new employees.

Offboarding: Process of managing transition when employee leaves.

Playbooks: Checklist of actions to perform to detect and respond to a specific type of incident.

# Governance Considerations

## Regulatory Considerations
Cover wide range of areas from data protection and privacy to environmental standards and labor laws (e.g., GDPR). Non-compliance can result in severe penalties.

## Legal Considerations
Closely tied to regulatory, but also encompass contract law, intellectual property, and corporate law.

## Industry Considerations
Specific standards and practices prevalent in a particular industry.

## Geographical Boundaries
Local, regional, national, and global regulations.
* Conflict of laws between different jurisdictions is a significant issue.

# Compliance

## Compliance Reporting
Systematic process of collecting and presenting data to demonstrate adherence to compliance requirements.
* **Internal**: Collection and analysis of data to ensure organization is following its internal policies and procedures.
* **External**: Demonstrates compliances to external entities like regulatory bodies, auditors, or customers.

## Compliance Monitoring
Process of regularly reviewing and analyzing and organizations operations to ensure compliance with laws, regulations, and internal policies.
* **Due Diligence**: Conducting an exhaustive review of an organization's operations to identify potential compliance risks.
* **Due Care**: Steps taken to mitigate these risks.
* **Attestation**: Formal declaration by a responsible party that the organizations processes and controls are compliant.
* **Acknowledgment**: Recognition and acceptance of compliance requirements by all relevant parties.

## Automation in Compliance
Can streamline data collection, improve accuracy, and provide real time compliance monitoring.

# Non-compliance Consequences

* **Fines**: Monetary penalties imposed by regulatory bodies for noncompliance with laws and regulations.
* **Sanctions**: Strict measures taken by regulatory bodies to enforce compliance.
* **Reputational Damage**: Negative impact on company's reputation.
* **Loss of Licenses**
* **Contractual Impacts**
