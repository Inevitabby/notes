title: "Physical Security"
---

Measures taken to protect tangible assets from harm or unauthorized access.

# Fencing and Bollards

Fencing prevent people from accessing your facility.
Bollards prevent vehicles from accessing your facility.

## Fence

Structure that encloses an area using interconnected panels or posts.
* Provides visual deterrent by defining a boundary of your property.
* Establish physical barrier against unauthorized entry.
* Delays buying your security personnel more time to react.
* Protects against trespassing, theft, vandalism, and unauthorized facility access.

## Bollard

Robust short vertical posts designed to manage or redirect vehicular traffic.
* Besides physically preventing vehicles going into pedestrian areas, they also are a clear visual indicator of where vehicles are not permitted.
* Keep vehicles away from buildings so people are out of range of IEDs.
* A bollard with an ASTM rating of F2656-07 M30 P1 means it can stop a 15k pound vehicle traveling 30 mph and prevent it from moving more than 1 meter after impact.

# Attacking with Brute Force

Brute force with respect to physical security is a direct and sometimes violent approach to bypassing security measures.

## Forcible entry

Physically breaking/bypassing barriers (e.g., windows, doors, fences).

Defense:
* Reinforced/laminated windows
* High-strength doors with deadbolt locks/metal frames/solid core
* Electric/barbed fence

## Tampering with security devices

Manipulating security devices.

Defense:
* Redundancy in physical security measures

## Confronting security personnel

Direct confrontation/attack with security personnel (e.g., weapons, distractions, etc.).

Defense:
* Rigorous training in conflict resolution and self-defense training
* Fast communication for backup and to signal potential security breach/attack

## Barrier ramming with vehicle

Use a vehicle to ram physical security barriers.

Defense:
* Bollards and other barriers

# Surveillance System

Organized strategy to observe and report a given area.
* Maintains the security and safety of a location.

## Video Surveillance

Provides real-time visual feedback and ability to review after an incident.
* Can also provide motion detection, night vision, facial recognition, remote access.
* Entrances and exits should be covered.
* Always record footage.

Wired/Wireless:
* **Wired**: Physically cabled back to the monitoring station.
* **Wireless**: Uses Wi-Fi to send signal back to central monitoring station. Easy to setup, but weaker to jam.

Indoor/Outdoor:
* An outdoor camera needs to be able to withstand the elements.

Pan-Tilt-Zoom (PTZ):
* Can move camera or its angle to better detect issues during an intrusion.

## Security Guards

* Very flexible and adaptable.
* Can reassure people they are safe.
* Also a visual deterrent.

## Lighting

Proper lighting is crucial for effective video and security guards surveillance.
* Also can deter criminals, or also serve as an alert system (e.g., motion activated).

## Sensors

### Infrared
Detect changes in infrared radiation. Infrared is emitted by warm bodies. Good for low light.

### Pressure
e.g., embedded in a floor/mat.

### Microwave
Detects movement by emitting microwave pulses and measuring their reflection off moving objects.
* Can cover large areas.
* Often configured with very high sensitivity level.

### Ultrasonic Sensors
Measures reflection of ultrasonic waves off moving objects.

### Acoustic Interference
Jamming or playing loud music to disrupt a microphone.

### Electromagnetic Interference
Jamming the signals systems rely on to monitor the environment (e.g., radio frequency jammers).

### Physical Environment Attack
Exploiting environment around surveillance equipment to compromise its functionality (e.g., increasing temperature of a room with an infrared sensor, targeting main power supply, starting a fire, etc.).

# Bypassing Surveillance Systems

1. **Visual Obstructing**: Blocking camera's line of sight.
2. **Blinding Sensors/Cameras**: Overwhelming sensor or camera with sudden burst of light to render it ineffective for a limited period of time.
3. **Physical Environment Attacks**

## Defending

Modern systems comes with defenses like:
* Tamper alarms
* Backup power supplies/UPS
* Frequency hopping and encrypted traffic to prevent eavesdropping

# Access Control Vestibule

Double-door system designed with two doors that ensures only one door can be opened at a given time.
* Often combined with other measures (e.g., access badges).
* Should be only big enough to let one person through at a time.
* The human component cannot be understated (security guards are needed for visual deterrent, providing assistance, manually giving temporary access badges to visitors, and responding to breaches).

# Piggybacking

Person with legitimate access intentionally allows an unauthorized person to enter with them.
* e.g., letting a delivery driver in.

# Tailgating

Unauthorized person follows someone with legitimate access through a secure space without their consent.

> Piggybacking vs Tailgating
> Piggybacking is a result of complicity or negligence, tailgating is done without knowledge or consent.

# Door Locks

## Pin & Tumbler

Pin and tumbler locks are easily defeated.

## Electronic Locks

Electronic door locks are less-easily defeated; e.g.,
* PIN
* NFC/RFID/Bluetooth locks
* Biometrics (e.g., facial, fingerprint)
	* **False Acceptance Rate (FAR)**: The rate a system falsely authenticates a person.
	* **False Rejection Rate (FRR)**: Rate system denies a user who should have been allowed access.
	* **Equal Error Rate (ERR)**: Measure of effectiveness of a given biometrics system to achieve a balance. Also known as Crossover Error Rate. We want as small an ERR as possible.

Per-person PIN/biometrics/etc. can also be used for auditing.
Combining multiple automated methods can increase security.

## Cipher Lock

Uses mechanical locking mechanism with push buttons that are numbered and require a person to enter a PIN.

# Access Badge Cloning

Access Badges: Fast to use, can provide lots of detailed information like access level.
* Technology: RFID, NFC, Magnetic Strips
* Every time an access badge is used, the action is logged. Good for building an audit trail.

Cloning:
1. Use a handheld RFID or NFC reader to capture data from a victim's card and store it for further processing.
2. Relevant authentication credentials are extracted.
3. Using specialized writing tools, extracted data is transferred to a blank RFID/NFC card.
4. Attacker can now use the cloned badge.

Cloning is a stealthy way to conduct a physical security attack.

Defense:
1. Implement advanced encryption in card-based authentication systems.
2. Implement Multi-Factor Authentication.
3. Regularly update security protocols.
4. Educate users.
5. Users should use shielded wallets/sleeves.
6. Monitor and audit the access logs.
