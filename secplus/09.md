Fundamental process that involves identifying, analyzing, treating, monitoring, and reporting risks.

# Risk Management Phases

1. **Risk Identification**: Proactive process with goal of creating a list of risks that may prevent organization from getting its objectives.
2. **Risk Analysis**: Evaluating likelihood and potential impact of risks. Can be qualitative or quantitative. Goal is to sort list of risks by priority.
3. **Risk Treatment**: Reduce risk to acceptable risk. Includes avoidance, reduction, sharing, acceptance.
4. **Risk Monitoring**: Ongoing process that requires tracking identified risks, assessing new risks, and evaluating the effectiveness of risk mitigation strategies.
5. **Risk Reporting**: Communicating risk and effectiveness of risk management process to stakeholders (e.g., dashboards, heat maps, detailed reports).


# Risk Assessment Frequency

Regularity with which an organization does risk assessment process. Varies greatly depending on organizational needs.

Types:
* **Ad-hoc**: Conducted as and when needed, often in response to an specific event or situation that has potential to introduce or change risk.
* **Recurring**: Conducted at regular intervals (e.g., annually/quarterly/monthly). Determined by organization policy.
* **One-Time**: Conducted for a specific purpose and are not reported (e.g., for a new IT system, construction project, or organizational change).
* **Continuous**: Ongoing monitoring evaluation of risks. Involves real-time data collection and analysis.

> **Aside**: Ad-hoc v. One-Time
> Ad-hoc: Specific events or situations and may be repeated.
> One-Time: Specific project or initiative and not repeated.

# Risk Identification

Recognizing potential risks that could negatively impact an organizations ability to operate or achieve its objects.
* Critical first step of Risk Analysis.
* Organization should consider a wide range of risks including operational, financial, strategic, and reputational.
* Risks should be analyzed based on probability and possible impact.

Techniques: Brainstorming, Checklists, Interviews, Scenario Analysis.

Often followed by Business Impact Analysis (BIA).

BIA: Process that involves evaluating potential effects of disruption to an organizations business functions and processes.

BIA terms:
* **Recovery Time Objective (RTO)**: Maximum acceptable length of time that can elapse before the lack of a business function severely impacts the organization.
* **Recovery Point Objective (RPO)**: Maximum acceptable amount of data loss measured in time (e.g., if your RPO is 15 minutes you need to back up every 15 minutes).
* **Mean Time to Repair (MTTR)**: Represents average time required to repair a failed component or system (how easy to repair a system is).
* **Mean Time Between Failures (MTBF)**: Average time between failures (how reliable a system is). e.g., if a system broke 5 times in a year, it would have an MTBF of ~72 days.

# Risk Register

Risk Register (Risk Log): Document detailing identified risks including their description, impact, likelihood, and mitigation strategies.
* Key tool in risk management, featuring key risk indicators, risk owners, and risk thresholds.
* Can resemble the heat map risk matrix.
* Should be shared among stakeholders.

Parts of Register:
* **Risk Description**: Identifies and provide detailed description of risk.
* **Risk Impact**: Potential consequence if risk materializes (cost/time/quality/etc.); usually rated low/medium/high.
* **Risk Probability**: Chance of risk occurring.
* **Risk Outcome**: Result of a risk, linked directly the impact and likelihood.
* **Risk Level**: Determined by combining the impact and likelihood.
* **Cost**: Pertains to financial impact on the project.
* **Risk Tolerance**: Organizations willingness to deal with uncertainty in pursuit of their goals.
* **Risk Appetite**: The amount of residual risk an organization is willing to accept.
	* Expansionary: More risk for greater returns.
	* Conservative: Less risk even if lower returns.
	* Neutral: Balance between risk and return.

Additionally we must know:
* **Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)**: Essential predictive metrics used by organization to signal rising risk levels in different parts of the enterprise.
* **Risk Owner**: Person/group responsible for managing the risk.

# Qualitative Risk Analysis

Method of assessing risks based on potential impact and likelihood of their occurrence.
* Subjective and relies on expertise and experience of project team and stakeholders.
* Likelihood: chance of risk occurring; typically qualitatively measured as low/medium/high.

# Quantitative Risk Analysis

Method of evaluating risk with numerical methods.

* **Exposure Factor (EF)**: Proportion of an asset that is lost in an event (0% no loss, 100% total loss).
* **Single Loss Expectancy (SLE)**: Monetary value expected to be lost in a single event (Value of Asset times EF).
* **Annualized Rate of Occurrence (ARO)**: Estimated frequency a threat is expected to occur within a year.
* **Annualized Loss Expectancy (ALE)**: Expected annual loss from a risk (SLE times ARO).

> **Example**:
> 
> Suppose a server is worth $10,000, the EF is 50%, and the server is expected to crash every two years.
> 
> $$ \text{SLE} = \text{Asset Value} \times \text{EF} = \$10,000 \times 0.50 = \$5,000 $$
> $$ \text{ALE} = \text{SLE} \times \text{ARO} = \$5,000 \times 0.5 = \$2,500 $$
> 
> If a vendor sells you a new server with a higher redundancy rating, you can use the formula to calculate savings to see if it is actually worth it.

# Risk Management Strategies

## Risk Transference (Risk Sharing)
Involves shifting risk from organization to another party (e.g., insurance policy, contract indemnity clause).
* Contract Indemnity Clause: One party agrees to cover other's harm, liability, or loss stemming form the contract.

Remember that moving financial burden of a potential loss does not eliminate the risk.

## Risk Acceptance
Recognizing a risk and choosing to address it when it arises.
* Used when the risk is unlikely to have a significant impact.

Exception: Temporarily relaxes security controls for operational business needs.[^exception-exemption]
Exemption: Permanent provision that grants an exception from a specific rule or requirement.

[^exception-exemption]: [Verified] Clarified the difference between Exception (temporary deviation) and Exemption (permanent deviation).

## Risk Avoidance
Strategy of altering plans or approaches to completely eliminate a specific risk.
* Used when the risk is too great.

## Risk Mitigation
Implementing measures to decrease the likelihood or impact of a risk.

# Risk Monitoring and Reporting

Risk Monitoring: Continually tracking identified risks, assessing new risks, and evaluating the effectiveness of risk mitigation strategies.

Residual Risk: Likelihood and impact after implementing mitigation/transference/acceptance measures on initial risk.

Control Risk: Assessment of how a security measure has lost effectiveness over time.

Risk Reporting: Process of communicating information about risk management activities.
* Informed decisions-making.
* Risk mitigation.
* Stakeholder communication.
* Regulatory compliance.
