# Good v.s. Bad Argument

Good Argument: Succeeds in proving its conclusion.
- 2 Components:
	1. True Premises
	2. Strong Inference
		- Pretend the premises are true, do they still lead to the conclusion?

Bad Argument: Fails to provide evidence for its conclusion.

# Implicit Premises

The implicit premise...
1. ... should narrow the logical gap between the stated premise and the conclusion.
2. ... shouldn't commit us to more than is necessaru.
	- e.g., "Sally can't go hiking because she has a broken leg" doesn't mean that "someone with a broken leg can't move".
