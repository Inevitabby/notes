# Chapter 2 Notes

## Definitions

Definitions: Tell us what class things go to by describing its class (genus) and essential characteristics (differentia).
- Functions:
	1. Paradigm/Prototype: Objects that are classified automatically by recognition
	2. Clarify Relation: Ostensive definition, "things like that"
	3. Summary Statement: Condenses knowledge we have on a class
- Complex definitions like "government" require a chain of definitions to understand. Weak chains mean poor understanding.
- Acceptable degrees of precision vary for context

## Genus and Differentia

**Genus**: Class of things regarded as have various subcategories.

**Differentia**: Differentiates subcategories of a genus from each other.
- Note: Can rarely be expressed as a single word. 

Example: "Humans are rational animals"
- Genus: Animals
- Differentia: Rational

## Rules for Definitions

1. Should include genus and differentia
	- Sign of genus omission in of a definition is "when" or "where"
		* e.g., "Fear is *when* you think you're in danger" misses the genus "emotion" with "when"
2. Neither too broad nor too narrow
	- Can be tested by finding counterexamples.
		* e.g., "Humans are religious animals" misses atheists.
