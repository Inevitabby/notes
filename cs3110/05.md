# Context-Free Grammar

Suppose the following CFG:

$$
S \to a S b \\~\\
S \to \epsilon
$$

S can render/derive/transform into $aSb$ or $\epsilon$

This is the parse tree of S:

![](.images/doodle_17.png)

> Why:
> $$
> S \to a S b \to aa S bb \to aaa S bbb \to aaa \epsilon bbb
> $$

In other words, this grammar can render anything in ${ a^n b^n }$, which is the language the grammar generates.
- Notation: $L(G)$ : **Language of the grammar**

> **Formal Definition of CFG**:
> $$
> G: (V, \Sigma, R, S)
> $$
> 
> - $V$: Finite set of variables 
> - $\Sigma$: Finite set of terminals
> - $R$: Set of rules
> - $S$: The start variable $(S \in V)$

> Q: What does the following CFG render?
> 
> $$
> S \to a S \\
> S \to S b \\
> S \to \epsilon
> $$
> 
> A:
> 
> This example doesn't enforce the # of as to be equal to bs (not {$a^n b^n$})
> 
> Renders: $\epsilon, a, b, ab, aa, bb, ...$
> 
> Thus: $L(G) = { a^* b^* }$

> Q: Define a CFG for {$a^{3n}$}
> 
> A: 
> 
> We want to generate the terminal in threes at a time, so:
> 
> $$
> S \to S aaa \\
> S \to \epsilon
> $$
> 
> shorter: $S \to S a^3 | \epsilon$

# NFA to CFG

- Look at the individual states and generate tiny grammars for them.
- This is essentially the ripping-out process.
- Accepting states go to epsilon.

> Q: Convert the NFA to CFG
> 
> ![](.images/doodle_18.png)
> 
> A: 
> $$
> Q1 \to a Q2 \\
> Q2 \to a Q3 \\
> Q3 \to a Q1 \\
> Q1 \to \epsilon
> $$

> Q: Convert the NFA to CFG
> 
> ![](.images/doodle_19.png)
> 
> A:
> $$
> S \to aA \\
> A \to  aS | bA | F \\
> F \to aA | bF | \epsilon
> $$

# Language to CFG

Strategy: Divide and conquer.

> Q: { $a^n ( b^3 )^n$ }
> 
> A: $S \to a S b^3 | \epsilon$

> Q: { $a^n ( bcd )^n$ }
> 
> A: $S \to a S bcd | \epsilon$
