# Introducing Yourself

## "So tell me a little bit about yourself"

This is not a good time to ad lib!

Think about [why]{.underline} they are asking you this:
1. For reassurance on your background
2. To spark conversation
3. To see what you think is important

Rough template: (starting points for crafting your own introduction)
1. Who are you?[^WHO] 
2. What's your background?[^BACKGROUND]
3. What's a recent accomplishment which fits this position?

[^WHO]: e.g., present position, field, major.

[^BACKGROUND]: e.g., education, interested, previous experience

> **Example**: Rough Template
> 
> 1. Who are you?
>   - I'm a 3rd year undergraduate at ...
>   - I'm a software engineer at ...
> 2. What's your background?
>   - I do ... at ...
>   - I'm interested in designing ... (emphasizing coursework)
> 3. Whats a recent accomplishment
>   - Last year in my such-and-such course project, my team designed ...
> 
> e.g., "I've just graduated from ... last May with my bachelor's in Computer Science. I'm very interested in designing .... Last year in my coursework for my team capstone project we built a ... from the ground up; with my main focus being the ..."

### Additional Criteria

- Be organized and brief (30 seconds — 1 minute)
  * Be well-rehearsed!
- Convey confidence
- Have a professional demeanor
- Make sure it's appropriate for the position[^APPROPRIATE]

[^APPROPRIATE]: It's very tempting to reuse a "good" introduction for everyone, but this is a mistake because you need to tailor it for each company!

## Common Pitfalls

> **Remember**: Your goal isn't to rehash all the content on your resume!

### Content

- It is very common to forget to introduce yourself / provide content.[^CONFCONT]
- Also, don't come off as overconfident!
- Remember to demonstrate team skills 

[^CONFCONT]: Confidence without content tells nothing!

### Demeanor

- Confidence
- Eye contact
- Fidgeting
- Poor posture
