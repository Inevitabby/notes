Problem: You have a function $f$ and you want to find.

$$
\int_a^b f(x) dx = ?
$$

> **Recall** — The trapezoidal approximation from Calculus I/II.
> 
> $$
> \frac{c_1 + c_2}{2} h \to 
> \frac{f(a) + f(b)}{2} (b-a)
> $$
> 
> Modify to take tiny trapezoids, yada yada yada. You've done this.
> 
> > **Note** — Work increases exponentially!

Q: 

$$
f(x) = x^2 + 3x + 1
$$

$$
x_0 = 1, x_1 = 2
$$

Find $\int f(x)dx$ from 1 to 2.

A:

**it1**:

$$
A_0 = \left[ 
	\frac{f(1) + f(2)}{1}
\right] (1) = 8
$$

**it2**: 

$$
A_1 = \left[ 
	\frac{f(1) + f(1.5)}{2}
\right] (0.5) = 3.1875
$$

$$
A_2 = \left[ 
	\frac{f(1.5) + f(2)}{2}
\right] (0.5) = 4.6875
$$

---

$$
A_1 + A_2 = 7.875
$$

**it3**:

$$
A_1 = \left[ 
	\frac{f(1) + f(1.25)}{2}
\right] (0.25) = 1.4140625
$$

$$
A_2 = \left[ 
	\frac{f(1.5) + f(1.75)}{2}
\right] (0.25) = 2.1328125
$$

$$
A_3 = \left[ 
	\frac{f(1.75) + f(2)}{2}
\right] (0.25) = 2.5390625
$$

$$
A_1 + A_2 + A_3 = 7.8437
$$

> Note — This will never hit the solution, unless you do this $\infin$ times.
