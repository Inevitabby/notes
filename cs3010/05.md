# Secant Method 

---

Derivation

We're going to modify this approximation of f'(x) used in the Secant method.

$$
f'(x) \approx \frac{f(xx+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

We'll pick/find some small $h$ and evaluate at x. To make it better, we'll split $h$ in half and approach x from both sides to sandwich.
- TODO What does approaching from both sides mean?

Thus, the sandwiching formula is

$$
f'(x_0) = \frac{f(x_0 + h/2) - f(x_0 - h/2)}{h}
$$

The smaller $h$ is, the better the approximation. Our stopping criteria will be when the delta falls below epsilon.

# Richardson's Extrapolation

---

Aside: Newton's Interpolation / Derivation

We could also use Newton's interpolation to solve this. Just truncate at the ???th term. 

The interesting thing is we can increase the terms to increase precision.

Richardson studied the behavior of this series (Newton's or the other?) and extrapolated the amount of error between iterations.

Basically, we use this to speed up the convergence.

"When you take out the error and plot it, there is a pattern. And if you know that, you can improve convergence of the original problem."
- Professor won't go into the derivation, but basically:

Suppose a function $P_i$ that gives us a series of estimates based on a given input. For us, last time, that function was this:

$$
\Phi_i = \frac{
	f(x_0 + h_i) - f(x_0 + h_i) 
}{
	2h_i
} \\~\\
h_i = h / 2^i
$$

(Last time we has the halfs on the inner h's, but we've moved them down for simplicity's sake)

The way our extrapolation works is: Suppose a function $D(i,j)$

When $j=0$, $D(i,j) = \Phi_i$. Otherwise, it's

$$
D(i,j) = D(i,j) + \frac{
	D(i,j-1) - D(i-1,j-1)
}{
	4^j - 1
}
$$

Properties:
- $i = 0,1,2,...$
- $j \le i$

**Example**: 

Q: 

$$
	f(x) = x^2 + \sin x, x_0 = 1.7, \epsilon = 0.00001
$$

$$
	h = 0.25
$$

> **Analytic Derivative** — Just so we have something to compare against, $f'(1.7) = 3.2711555...$.

A:

**Round 1**

$$
D(0,0) = \frac{
	f(x_0 + h_0) - f(x_0 - h_0)
}{
	2h_0
} \\
= \frac{
	f(1.7 + 0.25) - f(1.7 - 0.25)
}{
	0.5
} \\
= 3.27493448...
$$

$$
D(1,0) = \frac{
	f(1.7 + 0.125) - f(1.7 - 0.125)
}{
	0.25
} \\
= 3.271490776...
$$

Now we'll do something new, the extrapolation term.

$$
D(1,1) = D(1,0) + \frac{
	D(1,0) - D(0,0)
}{
	3
} \\
= 3.271490776 + 
\frac{
	3.271490776 - 3.27493448
}{
	3
} \\
= 3.271156552...
$$

> At the first round, we've already gotten more digits than the precious method at 5 iterations.

**Round 2**

$$
D(2,0)=3.271239372
$$

> I think this was pulled form the second method?

$$
D(2,1) = D(2,0) + \frac{D(2,0) - D(1,0)}{3} \\
= 3.271239372 + \frac{3.271239372 - 3.271490776}{3} \\
= 3.271155571...
$$

**Round 3**:

$$
D(2,2) = D(2,1) + \frac{D(2,1) - D(1,1)}{15} \\
= 3.271155571 + \frac{3.271155571 - 3.271156552}{15} \\
= 3.271155506
$$

> It has fully converged to the analytic derivative!
> - Now, we know we converged because we have the analytic derivative, but in the real-world, we wouldn't know when to stop. For that we'd do like at most five, or go until we start getting the same number with minor fluctuations (then we just pick one).
