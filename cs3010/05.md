# Secant Method 

---

Derivation

We're going to modify this approximation of f'(x) used in the Secant method.

$$
f'(x) \approx \frac{f(xx+h) - f(x)}{h}
$$

We'll pick/find some small $h$ and evaluate at x. To make it better, we'll split $h$ in half and approach x from both sides to sandwich.
- TODO What does approaching from both sides mean?
	* "In the definition of the derivative, you approach from ??? side."

Thus, the sandwiching formula is

$$
f'(x_0) = \frac{f(x_0 + h/2) - f(x_0 - h/2)}{h}
$$

The smaller $h$ is, the better the approximation. Our stopping criteria will be when the delta falls below epsilon.

**Example**: 

Q: 

$$
	f(x) = x^2 + \sin x, x_0 = 1.7, \epsilon = 0.00001
$$

> **Note** — Professor picked this polynomial because smooth polynomials converge really fast.

> **Analytic Derivative** — Just so we have something to compare against, $f'(1.7) = 3.2711555...$.

A: 

**It 1**: $h = 0.5$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'_1 (x_0) &= \frac{
	f(1.7 + 0.25) - f(1.7 - 0.25)
}{
	0.5
} \\
&= 3.27493448...
$$

**It 2**: $h = 0.25$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'_2 (x_0) &= \frac{
	f(1.7 + 0.125) - f(1.7 - 0.125)
}{
	0.25
} \\
&= 3.271490776...
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta &= |3.271490776 - 3.27493448| \\
&= 0.001002672 \not\lt \epsilon
\end{aligned}
$$

**It 3:** h = 0.125

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'_3 (x_0) &= \frac{
	f(1.7 + 0.0625) - f(1.7 - 0.0625)
}{
	0.125
} \\
&= 3.271239372...
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Delta &= |3.271239372 - 3.271490776| \\
	&= 0.000251... \not\lt \epsilon
	\end{aligned}
$$

**It 4**: $h = 0.0625$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'_3 (x_0) &= \frac{
	f(1.7 + 0.03125) - f(1.7 - 0.03125)
}{
	0.0625
} \\
&= 3.271176475...
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Delta &= |3.271176475 - 3.271239372| \\
	&= 0.000062897... \not\lt \epsilon
	\end{aligned}
$$

**It 5**: $h = 0.03125$

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'_3 (x_0) &= \frac{
	f(1.7 + 0.015625) - f(1.7 - 0.015625)
}{
	0.03125
} \\
&= 3.27116074834...
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
	\Delta &= |3.27116074834 - 3.271176475| \\
	&= 0.00001572... \not\lt \epsilon
\end{aligned}
$$

We'll stop here, but this is what the algorithm is. It's very simple to understand and implement.

> **Notes** — This tends to be slow to converge in general.
