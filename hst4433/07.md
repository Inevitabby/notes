title: "American Rage-Murder"
---

When individuals are subjected to extreme alienation and top-down cultures of fear, the resulting psychological pressure sometimes erupts into mass violence. Pundits often look for isolated answers, ignoring the institutional structures that cultivate this distinctly American phenomenon.

# Institutional Violence

**Rage-Murder**: Violent, often suicidal outbursts occurring in dominating institutions like workplaces and schools, driven by profound alienation, unpaid overtime, and cultures of fear.
- Frequently misattributed by pundits to video games, bad parenting, or isolated mental illness rather than institutional failures[^reaganomics].

**Idiographic vs. Nomothic Profiling**: Two distinct criminal profiling methods. Idiographic looks at a specific offender's unique traits (e.g., physical evidence, victimology), while nomothic examines commonly-inferred characteristics of groups of offenders.
- Both methods fail to grasp the root causes of rage-murder because they profile the individual rather than the violent institutions that push ordinary people to their breaking point.

> **Example**: The Banality of Misery in Schools and Workplaces
>
> School shootings and workplace massacres are two sides of the same coin. The environments deeply mirror each other physically—overhead fluorescent lights, industrial carpeting, identical cubicles and stalls—and psychologically. They both entrap individuals in a paradigm of surveillance and misery until violent suicide missions feel like the only way to make their profound disgruntlement visible.

[^reaganomics]: The socioeconomic context of these murders is tied to shifts like Reaganomics, which slashed benefits, deregulated industries, and framed poverty as a moral failure, drastically increasing the pressure on the working class.
