# Phrases

## Common

- **sumimasen**: Excuse me / I'm sorry
- **gomen-nasai**: I'm sorry
- **arigatou gozaimasu**: Thank you
- **onegai shimasu**: Please (requesting service/favor)
- **daijoubu desu**: I'm okay / It's fine
- **wakarimasen**: I don't understand

## Fill in the Blank

- **Where is (the)...**: __ wa doko desu ka?
	* any noun 
	* e.g., toire
- **I want to go to...**: __ ni ikitai
- **I am \_\_**: watashi wa __ desu
- **I can't eat \_\_**: __ ga taberarenai

## Transport & Directions

- **eki**: station
- **kono densha wa \_\_ ni ikimasu ka?**: Does this train go to \_\_?

## Food & Diet

- **osusume wa nan desu ka?**: What do you recommend?

## Money & Shopping

- **ikura desu ka?**: How much is it?
- **kore kudasai**: I'll take this
- **genkin / kaado**: cash / card

## Hotel & Emergencies

- **byouin wa doko desu ka?**: Where is the hospital?
- **tasukete kudasai**: Please help

## 2. Public Signs and Symbols

Public signage in Japan is designed to be clear and accessible. The following table not only provides translations but also explains where you might commonly see these symbols and what extra details they imply.

| Symbol / Text     | Meaning                   | Usage / Context                                          | Additional Notes                                                                                                |
|-------------------|---------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **トイレ**        | Toilet                    | Found in malls, parks, stations, and public areas        | Gender-specific signs may include 男 (men) and 女 (women); sometimes also rendered as お手洗い in formal places |
| **飲料水**        | Drinkable Water           | Located near rest areas, parks, and train stations       | Often accompanied by a water fountain icon                                                                      |
| **ポイ捨て禁止**  | No Littering              | Common across urban areas and parks                      | Helps maintain cleanliness—fines might apply                                                                    |
| **禁煙**          | No Smoking                | On public transport, in buildings, and restaurants       | Sign may include a crossed-out cigarette symbol                                                                 |
| **駐車場**        | Parking                   | Directs drivers to parking lots and garages              | Particularly useful near shopping centers or tourist sites                                                      |
| **指定席**        | Reserved Seat             | Seen on trains or buses                                  | Look for 自由席 for non-reserved seating                                                                        |
| **立入禁止**      | Do Not Enter              | Posted at restricted or hazardous areas                  | Often used in construction areas or private property zones                                                      |
| **非常口**        | Emergency Exit            | Vital in buildings, transport hubs, and public venues    | Commonly in glowing green, sometimes with a running figure icon                                                 |
| **神社** / **寺** | Shrine / Temple           | Found in neighborhoods, parks, and tourist spots         | 神社 (Shinto shrine) versus 寺 (Buddhist temple) — helps in cultural navigation                                 |
| **新幹線**        | Shinkansen (Bullet Train) | Marks stations and route maps related to high-speed rail | The term indicates Japan’s high-speed rail network                                                              |
| **コンビニ**      | Convenience Store         | Ubiquitous in urban and suburban areas                   | Many chains (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) do not use “コンビニ” in branding, but the function is the same      |
| **病院**          | Hospital                  | Locations on directional maps and building entrances     | Emergency services often noted separately, e.g., 救急                                                           |
| **円**            | Japanese Yen              | Seen on price tags, ATMs, and currency boards            | Key symbol for financial transactions                                                                           |
| **無料Wi-Fi**     | Free Wi-Fi                | Offered in cafes, major stations, and tourist hubs       | Look for the Wi-Fi icon alongside this text                                                                     |

## 3. Kanji in Context

Understanding common Kanji will help you decode signs, menus, and notices. Below is a curated list of frequent Kanji with practical examples and usage tips.

| Kanji | Meaning          | Context / Usage                                  | Additional Notes                                  |
|-------|------------------|-------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| 出口  | Exit             | Seen in train stations, malls, and buildings     | Often combined with arrow symbols                |
| 入口  | Entrance         | Provides a counterpart to 出口 in public buildings | Crucial when planning meeting points             |
| 駅    | Station          | Appears on transit maps, directories, and building signs | Identify train hubs and local transportation centers |
| 市    | City             | Used in addresses (e.g., 東京市 for Tokyo City)     | Helps decipher administrative regions            |
| 時    | Hour / Time      | Displays on clocks, event boards, and timetables   | Useful in schedules (e.g., opening times or departures) |
| 円    | Yen / Currency   | Found on signs related to prices and ATMs          | Integral for monetary transactions               |
| 禁止  | Prohibited       | Seen in combination with actions like smoking (禁煙)  | Indicates rules or warnings                       |
| 危険  | Danger           | Displayed in areas with construction or safety risks | Often highlighted with visual hazard symbols     |

## 4. Transit & Train

| Symbol / Text          | Meaning                      | Usage / Context                                                    | Additional Notes                                                                     |
|------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **電車**               | Train                        | Commonly displayed at station enclosures and timetables            | Includes different types (local, express, etc.)                                      |
| **駅**                 | Train Station                | Shown on maps, station names, and signage                          | Often featured with directional exit names like 東口 (East Exit) or 西口 (West Exit) |
| **JR**                 | Japan Rail (Japan Railways)  | Refers to the national network (e.g., JR Yamanote Line)            | Differentiates from private rail networks                                            |
| **私鉄**               | Private Railway              | For non-JR operators like Tobu, Keio, and Odakyu                   | Useful when traveling regionally                                                     |
| **出口 / 東口 / 西口** | Exit / East or West Exit     | Vital for finding your way out of large stations                   | Look for signage with arrows or directional maps                                     |
| **改札口**             | Ticket Gate                  | The entry point where you tap your transport IC card (Suica/PASMO) | Automated gates speed up your transit experience                                     |
| **のりかえ**           | Transfer                     | Indicates where to change lines or trains                          | Double-check maps to ensure correct platform transfers                               |
| **ホーム**             | Platform                     | Generally numbered (e.g., ホーム1, ホーム2)                        | Important for boarding; platforms are often announced in both Japanese and English   |
| **各駅停車**           | Local Train                  | Stops at every station along its route                             | Ideal for short urban trips                                                          |
| **快速**               | Rapid                        | Limited stops compared to local services                           | Check station lists as not all stations may be served                                |
| **特急**               | Limited Express              | Provides faster city-to-city travel; additional fare may apply     | Book in advance on services like the Shinkansen                                      |
| **自由席 / 指定席**    | Non-Reserved / Reserved Seat | A key aspect of train ticketing systems                            | Reserved seating provides guaranteed space, especially on long journeys              |

## 5. Sightseeing & Tourist

| Symbol / Text                | Meaning                    | Usage / Context                                                     | Additional Notes                                                      |
|------------------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **寺 (tera) / 神社 (jinja)** | Temple / Shrine            | Found in historical areas, cultural districts or tourist maps       | Temples usually indicate Buddhist sites while shrines are Shinto      |
| **入場券**                   | Admission Ticket           | Required for museums, parks, and special exhibits                   | Often includes a time limit or special event details                  |
| **撮影禁止**                 | No Photography             | Enforced in select areas like cultural sites or designated exhibits | Respect the rules to honor the site's traditions                      |
| **参道**                     | Shrine Approach / Path     | Describes the pathway leading up to a temple or shrine              | Often lined with lanterns, torii gates, or traditional shops          |
| **お手洗い**                 | Restroom (formal)          | Can be labeled as お手洗い, トイレ, or WC                           | Availability is usually indicated for visitor convenience             |
| **免税**                     | Duty Free                  | Marks shops or areas where tax-free shopping is available           | Look for these signs in areas like Akihabara or major tourist centers |
| **喫煙所**                   | Smoking Area               | Indicates designated smoking sections in public spaces              | Smoking indoors in many areas is strictly prohibited                  |
| **観光案内所**               | Tourist Information Center | Official spots at stations and major attractions                    | Staffed centers often provide maps and multilingual support           |
| **富士山**                   | Mt. Fuji                   | Used where Japan’s iconic mountain is featured or referenced        | Helps identify travel routes or scenic viewpoints                     |

*Add: **営業時間** (business hours). **定休日** (closed days). In transit hubs, look for **待合室** to find waiting areas.*
